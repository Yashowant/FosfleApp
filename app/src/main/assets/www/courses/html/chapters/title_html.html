Every webpage needs a name. Learn how the HTML <title> tag shapes your site's identity across browser tabs, bookmarks, and search engine results.

Section 01

What is a Page Title?

The HTML <title> tag defines the official title of the document. Unlike heading tags (like <h1>) which show up on the page itself, the <title> is meant for the browser and search engines. It must always be placed inside the <head> section.

ExampleHTML
<!DOCTYPE html>
<html>
<head>
  <!-- The title goes here -->
  <title>My Gallery Portfolio</title>
</head>
<body>
  <h1>Welcome to my art page!</h1>
</body>
</html>
Browser Output
My Gallery Portfolio

Welcome to my art page!

The title tag isn't visible here in the body.

Section 02

Where Do Titles Appear?

Since the title doesn't show up on the main page canvas, you might wonder why it's so important. The title is actually one of the most widely viewed elements of your website.

Browser Tabs

It's the text that appears on the tab at the top of your web browser, helping users find your site when they have many tabs open.

Search Engines

Google and Bing use the title tag as the large, clickable blue link in search engine results pages (SERPs).

Bookmarks & Shares

When a user bookmarks your site or shares the link on social media, the title tag is automatically used as the default name.

Section 04

Curation Guidelines

📏
Keep it around 50–60 characters If your title is too long, search engines will cut it off with an ellipsis (...). Keep it concise and front-load the most important words.
🏷️
Use a consistent format A common and effective format is: Primary Keyword - Secondary Keyword | Brand Name. For example: Men's Running Shoes | Nike.
🚫
Don't "Keyword Stuff" Avoid titles like Shoes, Buy Shoes, Cheap Shoes, Running Shoes. Search engines might penalize your site, and it looks untrustworthy to users.
✨
Make every page unique Never use a generic title like "Home" or "Page 1". Every single HTML document on your website should have a distinct, descriptive title.
Chapter Summary

Everything that stuck to the canvas

The <title> tag defines the title of an HTML document.

It must be placed exclusively inside the <head> section of your HTML.

The title is invisible on the webpage itself, but appears in browser tabs, search engine results, and bookmarks.

Titles are crucial for SEO (Search Engine Optimization) as they tell Google exactly what your page is about.

An ideal title is descriptive, unique to that specific page, and kept under 60 characters to prevent truncation.
