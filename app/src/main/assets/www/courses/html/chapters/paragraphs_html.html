HTML Paragraphs

A paragraph always starts on a new line, and is usually a block of text. In this chapter, we will learn how browsers calculate spacing, why they ignore your "Enter" key, and how to format blocks of text perfectly.

1

The HTML <p> Element

The HTML <p> element defines a paragraph. Browsers automatically add an empty line (called a margin) before and after a paragraph to separate it from other elements.

paragraphs.html 
<p>This is a paragraph.</p>
<p>This is another paragraph.</p>
Real Browser Output

This is a paragraph.

This is another paragraph.

2

HTML Display Behavior

You cannot be sure how HTML will be displayed. Large or small screens, and resized windows will create different results. However, the most important rule to remember is that browsers ignore extra spaces and new lines.

The Whitespace Collapse

If you put 10 empty spaces between two words in your HTML code, the browser will instantly delete 9 of them and only display 1 space. This is called "Whitespace Collapsing".

Ignoring the "Enter" Key

Pressing "Enter" or "Return" on your keyboard to create a new line in your text editor does absolutely nothing in the browser. The browser will print it all on one continuous line.

3

The Poem Problem

Because of the whitespace rules mentioned above, writing a poem or an address inside a single <p> tag will completely ruin the formatting. Look at what happens to this code:

broken-poem.html 
<p>
  My Bonnie lies over the ocean.
  
  My Bonnie lies over the sea.
  
  My Bonnie lies over the ocean.
  
  Oh, bring back my Bonnie to me.
</p>
Broken Browser Output

My Bonnie lies over the ocean. My Bonnie lies over the sea. My Bonnie lies over the ocean. Oh, bring back my Bonnie to me.

Notice how the browser flattened all the lines and spaces into one single, continuous sentence!

4

HTML Line Breaks (<br>)

The HTML <br> element defines a line break. Use <br> if you want a new line (a carriage return) without starting a new paragraph.

breaks.html 
<p>
  This is<br>
  a paragraph<br>
  with line breaks.
</p>
Real Browser Output

This is
a paragraph
with line breaks.

Empty Elements: The <br> tag is an empty element, which means that it has no end tag. You just type it and it immediately breaks the line!
5

The <pre> Element

If you are displaying computer code, ascii art, or a poem, placing <br> tags everywhere is exhausting. The HTML <pre> element defines preformatted text!

Text inside a <pre> element is displayed in a fixed-width font (usually Courier), and it preserves both spaces and line breaks exactly as you type them.

fixed-poem.html 
<pre>
  My Bonnie lies over the ocean.
  
  My Bonnie lies over the sea.
  
  My Bonnie lies over the ocean.
  
  Oh, bring back my Bonnie to me.
</pre>
Fixed Browser Output 
  My Bonnie lies over the ocean.
  
  My Bonnie lies over the sea.
  
  My Bonnie lies over the ocean.
  
  Oh, bring back my Bonnie to me.
6

Thematic Breaks (<hr>)

If you want to separate paragraphs or sections visually without starting a new heading, use the <hr> tag. It draws a horizontal line across the page.

This is the first paragraph of a story. It contains important details about the beginning.

This is the second paragraph. The horizontal rule above indicates a shift in time or a change of scene.

7

The Beginner Spacing Mistake

When a beginner wants to create extra blank space between two paragraphs on their website, they often try to cheat by spamming empty paragraph tags or line breaks like this:

<p>Paragraph 1</p>
<br><br><br>
<p></p>
<p>Paragraph 2</p>
Never do this!

HTML is purely for structure, not for visual styling. If you need 5 inches of blank space between two paragraphs, you must use CSS margins or padding. Screen readers get very confused when they read empty `

` tags out loud to visually impaired users.

8

Placeholder Text (Lorem Ipsum)

When professional developers are building a website, they often don't have the final text from the writers yet. Instead of leaving the webpage blank, they use a famous Latin placeholder text called Lorem Ipsum to test what the paragraphs will look like.

dummy-text.html 
<p>Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet, consectetur adipiscing elit. Sed do eiusmod tempor incididunt ut labore et dolore magna aliqua. Ut enim ad minim veniam, quis nostrud exercitation ullamco laboris nisi ut aliquip ex ea commodo consequat.</p>
VS Code Cheat Code!

If you are using a modern editor like VS Code, you don't need to copy and paste this from the internet. Just type lorem inside your <p> tag and hit the Tab key, and it will instantly generate a full paragraph for you!

Paragraphs Conquered!

You now understand exactly how the browser's whitespace parser works, how to override it with preformatted text, and how to create clean, accessible blocks of text.
